## 1. Verdict for a submission made today

**Would be rejected.** Three independent reasons, in the order a reviewer meets them:

1. **2.1(a) — cannot get in, metadata has placeholders.** There is no demo company and no reviewer login; the review notes still contain "☐ Ofir" placeholders.
2. **4.2 — the app opens on the desktop website in a web view.** One of three tabs is native. Sign-in, account, and the first screen a reviewer sees are web pages built for a desktop.
3. **2.3.1 / 2.1(a) — the listing describes things the app does not have as app screens** (Today, Lookup) and the notes describe the tabs incorrectly.

Behind those sit a group of plausible second-round rejections (privacy policy not easily reachable in-app, Support URL is a sales form, iPad never tested, reviewer can delete the demo company).

What is in good shape (read from code): the build is made with Xcode 26 as Apple requires, usage strings are specific, there is no tracking or ad SDK, the in-app billing page has no prices, account deletion exists and is reachable in every account state, the privacy manifest covers the data types, encryption flag is set.

## 6. Opinion — the minimum so 4.2 is not a realistic rejection

Apple's text is short: the app needs "features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website" and must be "app-like". A reviewer decides that in the first two minutes, from what they see at launch, at sign-in, and while moving between tabs. Today those two minutes are a desktop website. The fix is not to rebuild all 178 desktop screens; it is to make sure **a worker's whole day, and everything the reviewer is told to try, never touches a desktop page.**

**Must be native (SwiftUI):**
1. **Sign-in** — username, password, 2FA code, with Privacy and Terms links. Posts to the existing login endpoints and shares the cookie with the web views. This also removes the signed-out Pack problem (K7) and the "sign in on the Dashboard tab" instruction.
