# LiveOpsHub — AI Video Prompt Pack

Everything here is **paste-ready**. Generate shot by shot, then assemble in any editor.

**Two deliverables:**
- **Film A —** 75-second hero film, 16:9, for the top of the website
- **Film B —** 30-second vertical cutdown, 9:16, for Instagram / TikTok ads

---

## Before you start — read this once

**Generate one shot at a time.** Almost every AI video tool caps out around 5–10 seconds per clip. Trying to describe the whole story in one prompt produces mush. The shot list below is already cut to the right lengths.

**Keep the look identical across shots** by pasting the STYLE BLOCK at the top of every single prompt. That block is what makes 14 separate clips feel like one film.

**About the screens.** You chose fully-AI screens, so the shots below are written to work *with* that limitation rather than against it. AI video cannot render a real dashboard — it produces warped, gibberish text. So the shots deliberately show screens at an **angle**, in **shallow focus**, or as **glowing abstractions**: a green tick blooming, a scan line sweeping, bar charts rising as coloured light. That reads as "sophisticated software" and never as "broken text."

Two consequences worth knowing:
- **Don't ask for specific words on a screen.** Any prompt that says "the screen reads ORDER #4471" will come back as scrambled letters.
- **Put your real numbers in the edit, not the generation.** Add text overlays ("1,412 orders · 0 disputes") in your editor afterwards. They will be crisp, on-brand, and accurate.

If you later want the screens to be genuinely convincing, the single highest-impact upgrade is screen-recording the real app for 4 shots (7, 8, 11, 13) and compositing them onto the blank tablets. That is the difference between "nice ad" and "I want that."

## Tool adaptation notes

You said you haven't picked a tool yet. The prompts above are written to work broadly — here's what to change once you decide.

**Sora / Veo (rich narrative prompts)**
Use the prompts exactly as written; these tools reward the descriptive detail. You can also merge Shots 3+4 and 7+8 into single longer generations if your clip limit allows. Veo can generate audio — turn it off for everything except ambient warehouse tone, and never let it generate speech.

**Kling / Runway / Hailuo (shorter, more literal prompts)**
Trim each prompt to its first two sentences plus the camera move. These models drift when the prompt runs long. Add explicit negatives: `no text, no logos, no distorted hands, no extra fingers`. Generate 3–4 variants per shot and keep the best — hit rate is lower, so budget for it.

**Any image-to-video tool**
Strongest results overall: generate a still frame first (Midjourney / GPT Image), pick the best one, then animate it. You get far more control over the look and your 15 shots will match each other much more closely.

**Universal negative prompt:**
```
text, letters, words, captions, subtitles, watermark, logo, distorted hands, extra
fingers, warped faces, low resolution, oversaturated, fisheye distortion, jerky motion
```

## Assembly checklist

1. Generate all 15 shots. Keep 2–3 takes of Shots 7, 8, 10, 11 — the hero mechanics are worth choosing from.
2. Cut to the timings in the VO table. Hold the 4-frame black after Shot 4.
3. Add on-screen text in the edit (never in the generation).
4. Music: minimal electronic, steady pulse. Drop to near-silence at the black cut, rebuild from Shot 5, full energy by Shot 14.
5. Sound design earns more than you'd expect — the barcode beep on Shot 8 and the box-tape rip on Shot 11 are what make it feel real.
6. Export 16:9 at 1920×1080 for the site, 9:16 at 1080×1920 for social.
7. Drop the 16:9 file into `site/index.html` — replace the `.vframe` block with `<video controls poster="thumb.jpg" src="your-film.mp4"></video>`. The markup comment is already there.

## One honest caveat

AI video is excellent at atmosphere — warehouses, light, hands, motion — and poor at two things: **faces held in close-up for more than about 3 seconds**, and **anything with readable text**. Every shot above is built around that. If a face looks uncanny in a take, cut away sooner rather than trying to fix the prompt; you'll spend less time and the film will be tighter for it.
